What this campaign tries to give the players:

- **A consistent world.** One twist, applied rigorously: dragons are a fairy tale, and everybody acts like it.
- **Fascinating lore, but not overwhelming.** The secret history exists to make the world feel deep. Players discover it in fragments. They should never sit through a lecture.
- **Intriguing places to explore.** A sewer with a mob boss, a crypt nobody was meant to open, a reef shaped like something in flight.
- **Memorable characters to meet and defeat.** Every NPC has a hook: a voice, a smell, a secret.
- **Challenging and rewarding encounters.** Fights have tactics, terrain and a way out.
- **Innovative and useful treasures** that spice up both combat and role-play.

## The Recurring Question

Again and again the campaign puts the players in front of something that *might* be a dragon:

- a weathered statue
- a puppet in a duke's dungeon
- an "invisible dragon" in a clearing
- a pair of daggers said to be made of claws

Each time they have to decide what's real. The most important clue is that the world insists none of it is.
