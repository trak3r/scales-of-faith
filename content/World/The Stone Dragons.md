*Strange statues in strange places.*

## What People See

Scattered across the world are statues of exquisite detail: a boulder in a forest, a reef offshore, an idol in a jungle temple. Most are weather-worn, covered in lichen, moss, coral or seaweed. Few people look closely, and those who do assume some ancient sculptor had a taste for the fantastic.

> [!secret]- GM Only
> **They are the dragons themselves**, petrified by [[The Stoning]] exactly as they were in that instant. See the [[Stone Dragon]] bestiary entry.
>

> | The reef | [[Dragon Reef]] | Underwater, posed in flight, covered in coral and seaweed |
> | The winged snake | [[U'gachaka]] | Worshipped by a jungle tribe; legs hidden under offerings |
> | The obsidian dragon | [[Lake of Fire]] | Atop a volcano in a lake of lava; rumored obsidian and perfect |
> | The ice dragon | [[The Glacier]] | Revealed by a melting glacier |
> | The cliffside outcrop | Coastal cliffs ([[Sea Voyage]]) | A rock formation that resembles a dragon |
>
> **Fakes and red herrings:**
> - [[The Excavation]]: a crude, man-carved head.
> - [[Puppetmaster]]: a puppet in a duke's dungeon.
> - [[Gordon's Dragon Experience]]: an "invisible dragon" that turns out to be a bulette.
>
> **Finding them.** The Order keeps historical records of lairs. The spell *Dragon's Direction* (see [[Spells]]) and the [[Dragon Compass]] give vague bearings. Folk tales and tall tales, like [[Brock Blusterhaus]]'s, sometimes point the way.
>
> **Reviving them.** The [[Calla Cabal]] can revive a stone dragon. To soften the shock, it opens a portal to [[The Plane of Dreams]] *before* the revival, with a previously rescued dragon present to help the newcomer adjust.
